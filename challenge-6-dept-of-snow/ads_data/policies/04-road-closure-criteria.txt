ROAD CLOSURE CRITERIA - Alaska Department of Snow (fictional agency)
Document ADS-POL-004. Effective 2026-10-01.
Fictional agency and policy created for an ADK training exercise.

PURPOSE
This policy says when the Alaska Department of Snow (ADS) closes a road, who
decides, and what must happen before the road reopens. It applies to every
road priority class: P1 (highways and life-safety routes), P2 (arterials and
main collectors), and P3 (residential, rural, and seasonal roads).

WHEN TO CLOSE A ROAD
Close a road when any one of these is true.
- Visibility is under 1/4 mile for 30 minutes.
- Avalanche debris blocks a lane.
- A crash blocks a lane.
- Avalanche danger is High on an avalanche-prone segment.

AVALANCHE-PRONE SEGMENTS
ADS lists two avalanche-prone segments.
- Seward Highway, Turnagain Pass
- Hatcher Pass Road
When avalanche danger is High on either segment, the segment is closed.

WHO DECIDES
- Only the Regional Duty Supervisor can close or reopen a road.
- Each of the four regions (Anchorage, Mat-Su, Kenai, Fairbanks) has a
  Regional Duty Supervisor, who decides for roads in that region.
- Dispatchers, plow operators, and AI assistants can report conditions and
  recommend a closure. They cannot close or reopen a road themselves.

REOPENING
- Reopening needs a plow pass and an inspection.
- After both are done, the Regional Duty Supervisor decides whether to reopen.
- A road stays closed until the supervisor reopens it, even if conditions
  look better.

CLOSURE STEPS
1. Field staff or a dispatcher reports the condition to the Regional Duty
   Supervisor.
2. The supervisor checks it against the criteria above and decides.
3. If the road is closed, a Closure alert is drafted and approved by the
   supervisor before it is published (see ADS-POL-005, Public Alert
   Standards).
4. To reopen: plow pass, then inspection, then the supervisor's decision.

WORKED EXAMPLE
A plow operator reports visibility under 1/4 mile on a highway segment at
2:10 PM. At 2:40 PM visibility is still under 1/4 mile, so the 30 minute
criterion is met. The dispatcher recommends a closure, and the Regional Duty
Supervisor closes the road. Later the wind drops. The road reopens only after
a plow pass, an inspection, and the supervisor's decision to reopen.
