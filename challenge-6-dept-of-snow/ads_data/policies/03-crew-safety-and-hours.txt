CREW SAFETY AND HOURS OF SERVICE - Alaska Department of Snow (fictional agency)
Document ADS-POL-003. Effective 2026-10-01.
Fictional agency and policy created for an ADK training exercise.

PURPOSE
This policy protects plow, grader, snowblower, and sander operators at the
Alaska Department of Snow (ADS) from fatigue and extreme cold. It applies in
every region and at every storm level.

SHIFT LENGTH AND REST
- Shifts are at most 12 hours.
- Each shift is followed by at least 10 hours of rest before the operator's
  next shift.
- Dispatchers track every operator's hours on shift.

OPERATORS NEAR THE END OF A SHIFT
- An operator with 10 or more hours on shift may only take assignments
  expected to end before 12 hours on shift.
- If the assignment would run past 12 hours, give it to another operator.
- An operator with fewer than 10 hours on shift can take any assignment, but
  the shift still ends at 12 hours.

EXTREME COLD
- No solo operation when wind chill is -40 F or colder.
- At -40 F wind chill or colder, each vehicle works with a second crew member
  or alongside a second vehicle.

WHAT DISPATCHERS CHECK BEFORE ASSIGNING
1. The operator's hours on shift.
2. How long the assignment is expected to take.
3. The wind chill on the segment.
If any check fails, assign a different operator or wait for one to come on
shift. Crew safety limits are not waived to meet a clearing target.

WORKED EXAMPLE
An operator has 11 hours on shift. Two jobs are waiting.
- A plow pass expected to take 2 hours would end at 13 hours on shift. The
  operator cannot take it.
- A short pass expected to take 30 minutes would end before 12 hours on
  shift. The operator may take it, then goes off shift for at least 10 hours
  of rest.
