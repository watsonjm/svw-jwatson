EMBER OPTICS - ERROR CODES AND TROUBLESHOOTING
Fictional company and products created for an ADK training exercise.
Applies to Ember 256, Ember 384 LRF, and Ember 640 LRF.

ERROR CODES
E1  Battery too cold. Below -4 F (-20 C) the unit protects the cell. Warm the
    battery in a pocket for 10 minutes, then restart.
E2  Battery voltage low or wrong cell. Use a protected 18650 rated 3,000 mAh
    or higher with a button top. Flat-top cells do not make contact.
E3  Shutter stuck. Hold Power and M together for 5 seconds to force a shutter
    cycle. If E3 returns, send the unit for repair.
E4  Sensor over temperature. The unit was pointed at the sun or a fire. Turn
    it off for 15 minutes. Repeated E4 can leave a burn mark on the sensor.
E5  Storage full. Delete files in the EmberLink app or connect by USB-C.
E6  Storage error. Back up your files, then format: full menu > System >
    Format Storage.
E7  Rangefinder misaligned (384 LRF and 640 LRF only). Run the boresight
    check in the Calibration and Maintenance guide.
E8  Rangefinder no return. Nothing within range reflected the laser. Common
    when ranging sky, water, or through rain. Not a fault.
E9  Wi-Fi module fault. Toggle Wi-Fi off and on. If it repeats, update
    firmware to 2.4 or later, which fixed a Wi-Fi start-up bug.
E10 Firmware update failed. Keep the battery above 50 percent and retry from
    the EmberLink app. The previous firmware stays installed.
E11 Compass not calibrated (640 LRF only). Full menu > Compass > Calibrate,
    then turn the unit in a figure-eight for 10 seconds.
E12 Internal fault. Note the code and serial number and contact Ember
    Optics service.

COMMON PROBLEMS
- Image is grainy or speckled: run a manual shutter calibration (tap Power
  and Up together) and switch to the Open or Rain/Fog scene mode.
- Image is blurry: turn the focus ring on the lens. Thermal focus changes
  between 50 and 200 yards.
- Screen is too bright at night: full menu > Display > Brightness, or use
  the Black Hot palette.
- Unit will not turn on: check the battery orientation (positive end toward
  the lens) and try a fully charged cell.
